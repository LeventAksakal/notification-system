# Verifier report: shared provider health (15 Nov 2026)

Fresh-context `verifier` on commits `6264432`, `d30265c` (branch `claude/feat-shared-provider-health-d8f5a2`). Verbatim findings, then how each was handled (added by the main thread).

Verdict: FAIL. One high finding: a partial loss of the Valkey state can stop a channel for good. Also the new B1/R12 acceptance tests were never written, and the worker rate integration test failed (probably the loaded host, not proven).

## Findings, most severe first
1. **HIGH: a worker stops sending on a channel for good when the Valkey state goes back to an older generation.** `internal/delivery/health/gate.go:96` ignores any reply with a lower generation. `gate.go:90` treats a closed ticket as current only if the cached generation equals the ticket's. A closed state never moves its generation (`valkey_state.go:58`; only a declaration, probe or cancel bumps it). Scenario: Valkey crashes and reloads its last RDB snapshot, or fails over to a replica that is behind. The compose `valkey` service runs the image defaults, which save RDB snapshots to `/data`. If an outage, recovery or probe cancel happened after the snapshot, every worker that saw it holds generation Gn, larger than the restored G0. Then `Allow` hands out tickets for G0 and `Current` always says no, so `delivery.go:483` gives the whole grant up and loops again at once (`continue`, no sleep) for every reservation. Nothing is sent, so no generation ever moves. The only sign is the `breaker_opened` give-up metric; nothing is logged. The record's line "a worker's cached view never sticks" holds only for a full wipe on a clock that never steps back; the contract test's `wipe` always moves the clock forward, so this is untested. Fix: pass the gate's cached generation to `admit`, and have the script set gen = max(now, cached+1) when the stored gen is lower; or let an Admit reply overwrite a lower cached generation.
2. **HIGH (missing tests):** the acceptance tests for B1 and R12 are only drafted, and the env patch for the existing suite is not applied. `test/acceptance/` still sets the removed `APP_BREAKER_WINDOW/_MIN_REQUESTS/_FAILURE_RATIO`, which `config.Load` ignores, so K=5 now applies. At risk: `delivery_crash_test.go:27` (timeouts may now declare an outage) and `delivery_gaps_test.go:217` (its accounting is under the old rule). `test/acceptance/` itself is unchanged from main, so nothing was weakened.
3. **MEDIUM: five or more message-specific failures due together trip a fleet-wide outage again and again.** Rows excused by an outage are all rescheduled to the next probe time (`failure_attribution.go:43`), so they come due as a bunch. After each recovery the bunch fails K times in a row and declares again. Each round stops the channel for at least 5 s and counts at most 4 units across the bunch, so N bad rows take about 10N/4 rounds before they are dead-lettered (N=10: about 25 rounds, 2 minutes or more with the channel mostly shut). Fix: spread excused due times over a cool-down, and add a test with N ≥ K bad rows.
4. **MEDIUM: an outage can dead-letter a message, against "dead-letters nothing":** the K-1 counted failures can land on a message with K-1 or fewer units left; a report that cannot reach Valkey counts (`failure_attribution.go:66`); `APP_MAX_ATTEMPTS < APP_BREAKER_FAILURES` is allowed (only the defaults are checked; the R12 retry test uses 3 against 5), and then a lone message is dead-lettered before its outage is detected. Fix: have config refuse MaxAttempts < BreakerFailures, and correct the claim in the record.
5. **LOW: two probes can be out at once.** `probeLease` does not cover the wait for `cr.claiming`, a slow claim, or a report slower than 250 ms. Once the lease runs out a second worker probes, and the first still sends: its cached generation still matches. Cost: one extra call; its late failure counts 1 unit if the second probe already recovered. Fix: refuse the probe send after admission + lease.
6. **LOW:** a high-priority poison row gets about 70% of probes (weighted round robin, `priority.go:61`), so a lower-priority good row waits several cool-downs, not one.
7. **LOW (record wording):** "K failed calls per wipe" and "declared within 5 failed calls (53 ms)" count reports, not calls sent: up to Horizon+SendAlign worth of slots (about 38 at 95/s), or every free call place during a hang, go out first; the budget count of K-1 is right. "About 360 probes in 30 min" is an upper bound. 0007 still says "12 attempts" in its Decision section, and its "As built" section cites `TestTheDefaultRetryBudgetOutlastsATenMinuteOutage` and a start-up `retry_span` log, neither of which exists on this branch.

## Checked and sound (verifier)
One atomic script per step; exactly one probe per cool-down; stale or late probe results ignored by generation. A declaration counts exactly K-1 units under any order of replies; failures in flight at the declaration and probe failures are excused. Each worker stops by its next `Allow` (refill 100 ms plus g.Wait), none of those calls count. A full wipe mid-outage re-declares after K faults. The rate cap holds after recovery because every send, the probe included, takes a limiter slot. Every number recomputes (K=5; false trips at p=10/20/30% every 19.5 min / 41 s / 6.2 s; 720 probes/h = 0.21% of 342 000; probeLease 5.6605 s; B=10; spans 810 s / 405 s; 0, 1, 3, 7, 15 s) and the code uses them as stated. Migration matches `schema/`; replay resets `budget_spent`; every write keeps `budget_spent <= attempts`.
