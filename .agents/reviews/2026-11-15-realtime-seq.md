# Verifier: realtime-seq (status_seq ordering, readiness on MySQL only), 15-16 Nov 2026

A fresh-context `verifier` (read-only) ran for 71 min against commits 46aefb6 and 8127ae4 plus the uncommitted docs. It was briefed with the owner's objective and an attack list, not with the lane's reasoning. Its report is summarised faithfully below; handling is in the right-hand column.

Verdict: FAIL (one blocker; the delivery package fails under -race). Objectives 2 (readiness) and 3 (numbers) held, apart from the notes.

| # | Severity | Finding (verifier's words, condensed) | Handling |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | blocker | `seq_reorder.go`: a track was deleted one hold after its sent or cancelled event was passed on. A late predecessor arriving after that got a fresh unseeded track and was shown after the newer state. Trace: the release/reclaim race test with a 2.5 s delay; sent forgets at T+2.005, pending(3) lands at T+2.5 and is written after sent. Established by reading; the test's own 1.5 s case shows that timed-out publishes still land. | Fixed. | 2 | should-fix | The unfiltered stream's memory had no bound: tracks were dropped only after sent, cancelled or unseeded failed, so scheduled, pending and retrying notifications stayed for the socket's life. | Fixed. An unseeded track is forgotten `forgetUnseededAfter` (16 min) after its last event, whatever its status. The window is derived from TCP's retransmission give-up (Linux `tcp_retries2 = 15`, about 924.6 s). Test `TestReordererForgetsQuietUnseededNotificationsWhateverTheirStatus`. |

The 1.5 s integration case, whose publish times out but whose bytes land, now shows every state in order. |
